# Epicureanism Demo Sources

This demo uses short public-domain source excerpts for teaching behaviour only. It is intentionally small.

## Source 1: Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus

Reference: Internet Classics Archive, `Letter to Menoeceus` by Epicurus.  
URL: https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html

Key excerpt:

> The end of all our actions is to be free from pain and fear.

Teaching use:

- Use this first when explaining why Epicurean pleasure is not indulgence.
- Connect pleasure to freedom from bodily pain and mental disturbance.

## Source 2: Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus

Reference: Internet Classics Archive, `Letter to Menoeceus` by Epicurus.  
URL: https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html

Key excerpt:

> We do not choose every pleasure, but sometimes pass over many pleasures, when a greater annoyance ensues from them.

Teaching use:

- Use this when a student equates pleasure with chasing every enjoyable experience.
- Emphasise choice, calculation, and long-term tranquillity.

## Source 3: Epicurus, Principal Doctrines

Reference: Internet Classics Archive, `Principal Doctrines` by Epicurus.  
URL: https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/princdoc.html

Key excerpt:

> Pleasure in the body admits no increase when once the pain of want has been removed; after that it only admits of variation.

Teaching use:

- Use this to explain the limit of pleasure.
- Distinguish stable satisfaction from increasing luxury.

## Source 4: Epicurus, Principal Doctrines

Reference: Internet Classics Archive, `Principal Doctrines` by Epicurus.  
URL: https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/princdoc.html

Key excerpt:

> Of all the things which wisdom provides to make us entirely happy, much the greatest is the possession of friendship.

Teaching use:

- Use this for questions about friendship and community.
- Correct the misunderstanding that Epicureanism is selfish isolation.
